# The misconception of holiness 

Too often do we mistake the visual language of preservation as a reflection of holiness: marble apostles sealed behind velvet ropes and varnished artworks hanging in climate-controlled silence. All resting in a space seemingly detached from the rest of the world. Sanctity is achieved with stillness, reaching a place where nothing decays.

But nothing breathes, either.

The world *never* stops moving, and so perhaps holiness is better understood as constant recalibration. The act of staying upright while the ground shifts beneath us. The challenge lies not in achieving perfect stillness, but in responding to change without losing our orientation. 
# Rigidity invites fracture


# Balance is Iterative
If we assume that the world is in constant motion, then we accept the following: stillness is not true stillness. It's a constant state of micro-adjustments made in real time to accept the change as they come. When we maintain our posture, we correct for gravity, for uneven grounds, for the wind that changes direction as spontaneously as it arrives. And yes--sometimes we falter, but the grace in this endeavor is not found in the ability to achieve and perfect it, but rather the pursuit itself.
